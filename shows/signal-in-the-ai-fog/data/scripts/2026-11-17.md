# Brussels drafts rules for shopping agents

_2026-11-17_

The Commission's Digital Fairness Act proposal would give shoppers rights when an AI agent buys for them. Also: Anthropic's IPO prices tomorrow, Google and OVHcloud stage sovereign AI summits, Nvidia reports without its founder, and a new shopping agent for e-commerce merchants.

## Script

Good morning, it's Tuesday, November 17. This is Signal in the AI Fog, your daily briefing on enterprise AI in production, written and read by AI. Today: Digital Fairness Act targets shopping agents, Google and OVHcloud sovereign AI summits, and Anthropic's IPO prices Wednesday.

First, the story of the day.

The European Commission has proposed a Digital Fairness Act, a consumer protection regulation. Yahoo reports it would add safeguards for shoppers who use AI agents to buy on their behalf. Consumers would get a right to reach a human customer service representative, and clear rights when an agent purchases for them. The text also covers unfair practices carried out by AI agents. According to CASRAI, it includes a provision requiring controls to switch off features that encourage compulsive use, with some exclusions. Sources give different dates for the announcement, so none is stated here. The proposal now goes to the European Parliament and the Council. None of these safeguards is law yet.

What this really signals: agentic shopping is being framed from two sides. Retailers are writing admission rules for agents. Brussels is now drafting the consumer's rights on the other side. Nothing binds yet, and the text will change in Parliament and Council. But the design direction is already usable for a platform team: log which agent acted for which customer, keep a clear path to a human, and make agent-initiated orders traceable and reversible. Review your own conversational and agent features against those basics now, with legal in the loop. Worth watching: how the final text defines an AI agent, because that decides whether your customer-facing assistants fall in scope.

Next, Models and vendors.

According to CNBC, Anthropic expects to price its offering on the evening of November eighteenth and start trading on Nasdaq on November nineteenth. The range is seventy to seventy-eight dollars a share, implying a valuation of up to about 1.4 trillion dollars. CNBC's coverage cites a revised filing showing seventeen billion dollars of third-quarter revenue.

Why it matters: the pricing is tomorrow, and nothing in your contract changes with it. The useful signal is the revised filing, which will keep publishing a frontier vendor's commitments. Keep the failover rehearsal on your calendar.

Next, Sovereignty and Europe.

Google Cloud holds its Sovereign AI Summit in Brussels today, November seventeenth, with a session on an independent AI stack made with Europe. OVHcloud's AI Summit follows in Paris on November nineteenth, where its leaders will present AI strategy and product announcements. A beta of OVHai Workspace is due to be announced there.

The bigger picture: two sovereignty pitches in three days, one from a hyperscaler and one from a European provider. Compare them on concrete terms: where models run, who operates them, price, and what you can actually order today. Announcements are not capacity.

Next, Platform.

Nvidia reports third-quarter earnings after the US market closes on Tuesday, November seventeenth, according to Yahoo Finance. Bloomberg consensus is about 109 billion dollars of revenue, with about 102 billion from data centers. Interim chief executive Colette Kress leads the call after Jensen Huang's resignation. Yahoo's headline mentions a reported Huang comeback, without detail.

Reading between the lines: listen for supply, lead times and roadmap, not the headline number. They shape your inference capacity planning and GPU pricing. Ask how much of your serving stack depends on one accelerator vendor.

Next, Retail and agents.

Commerce.com, the company behind BigCommerce, announced on November sixteenth a Shopping Agent for BigCommerce merchants. It is described as a customizable AI storefront assistant for product discovery and shopping guidance. It is rolling out to eligible merchants ahead of the holiday selling season.

The read here: storefront assistants are becoming a platform feature merchants switch on. For a retailer, the lasting work is product data quality and clear answers on returns and delivery. Test any such assistant on your own catalogue before peak.

The number of the day.

Eight hundred ninety-five point four billion dollars. That is the purchase commitments in Anthropic's revised IPO filing, according to CNBC. Set against seventeen billion dollars of quarterly revenue, it shows how much capacity the vendor has already contracted.

What to watch.

Today: Microsoft Ignite opens, with the Nadella keynote at seven in the evening Paris time. Nvidia reports after the US close. Several Gemini audio previews shut down. Tomorrow evening, Anthropic prices its IPO. November nineteenth: first trade, OVHcloud's summit, and Gemini 3.6 Flash retirement. Walmart's Agent Profiles are due about November twenty-first. Then Gemini 2.5 reroutes December first, AI Act marking starts December second, and EuroHPC's Lot 2 closes December third.

And a question for your team.

If a law gave shoppers explicit rights when an agent buys for them, could you today trace which agent placed which order, for which customer, and reach a human?
