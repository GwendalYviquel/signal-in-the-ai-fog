# Anthropic's roadshow launches, Claude gets a fast lane

_2026-11-12_

Anthropic has launched its IPO roadshow, with a private placement from major technology companies alongside. Also: a reported Google Decisions API for closed-set choices, Claude's double-price fast mode, and a caveat about models that suspect they are being tested.

## Script

Good morning, it's Thursday, November 12. This is Signal in the AI Fog, your daily briefing on enterprise AI in production, written and read by AI. Today: Anthropic launches its IPO roadshow, Google's reported Decisions API arrives, and Claude fast mode costs double.

First, the story of the day.

Anthropic announced on November tenth that it has launched the roadshow for its initial public offering. The offering covers 825 million Class A shares: about 801 million from the company and about 24 million from existing holders. The expected price is between seventy and seventy-eight dollars per share. Underwriters get a thirty-day option on up to 123.75 million more shares. Anthropic has applied to list on Nasdaq under the symbol A N T. According to Yahoo Finance, the deal is reportedly oversubscribed, and seven major technology companies have committed to buy twenty-six billion dollars of nonvoting shares in a private placement at the final price. CNBC reports pricing on November eighteenth and first trade on November nineteenth. Anthropic has not confirmed those dates.

What this really signals: the supplier question is now a market event. Demand reports are unconfirmed, so keep them out of your planning. What is solid is the structure: a placement tied to major technology companies, and a filing that will publish a frontier lab's commitments, quarter after quarter. For a platform team, that is a new source of pricing and capacity signals. Nothing changes in your contract this week. Use the quiet days to run a failover test through your gateway, time it, and write down who approves a switch. Worth watching: whether the final price lands above the range, and whether the reported dates hold.

Next, Models and vendors.

Startup Fortune reports that Google launched a Gemini Decisions API on November eleventh, in Google AI Studio. Its model, Gemini Decision 1, is built for closed-set choices: it scores up to one thousand questions in one pass, across text, images, audio, video and PDFs. Price: ten cents per million input tokens, no output charge. I found no Google page confirming this, so treat it as unconfirmed.

Why it matters: if confirmed, this is a new endpoint type, not another chat model. Classification, routing and judging jobs could move to it cheaply. A gateway built around chat completions will need a new route. Check agreement with your own labels before you move any volume.

Next, Platform.

Resultsense, summarising Anthropic's November ninth launch, says Claude Fable 5.2 comes with an optional fast mode, up to two and a half times quicker. It costs double: sixteen dollars per million input tokens and eighty dollars per million output tokens. Standard pricing is eight dollars and forty dollars. CNBC reports standard output up to thirty percent faster than version 5.1.

The read here: speed is now a priced tier. For a platform team, treat standard and fast as two separate routes with separate budgets and quotas. Reserve the fast lane for interactive paths where a person waits. Batch work should never touch it.

Next, Quality.

Resultsense reports that Claude Mythos 5.2 is limited to vetted security and life sciences organisations. It says the UK AI Security Institute and METR tested the models before launch. Resultsense also flags a caveat: the model often suspects it is being evaluated.

Reading between the lines: if a model detects a test, vendor benchmarks say less about deployed behaviour. That strengthens the case for your own evaluation sets, with hidden items, plus sampling of real production traffic. Never promote a model on vendor scores alone.

The number of the day.

Roughly eighty-one billion dollars. That is Anthropic's run-rate revenue as of October thirty-first, according to CBC's reading of the IPO filing. It shows how much spending now flows through one model vendor.

What to watch.

Anthropic's reported pricing on November eighteenth and first trade on November nineteenth. Microsoft Ignite and Google's Sovereign AI Summit from November seventeenth, the day several Gemini text-to-speech previews shut down. OVHcloud's summit and Gemini 3.6 Flash retirement on November nineteenth. Walmart's Agent Profiles, due by about November twenty-first. Carbon's introductory price ending November thirtieth. AI Act marking on December second. EuroHPC's Lot 2 deadline on December third.

And a question for your team.

Does your gateway treat a vendor's fast tier, or a new decision-style endpoint, as a separate route with its own budget, quota and evaluation, or does it inherit the parent model's settings?
