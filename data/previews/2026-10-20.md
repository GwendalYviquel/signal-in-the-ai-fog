# Argon's price clock, Mistral's weights, and agents at checkout

_2026-10-20_

Google's Gemini 4 Argon is generally available at an introductory price that doubles on December first. Also: Mistral Large 4 weights are due by month end, NVIDIA open-sources Run:ai, the AI Act's December dates, and Google's Universal Cart brings agentic checkout to retailers.

## Script

Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 20. This is Signal in the AI Fog, your daily briefing on enterprise AI in production, written and read by AI. Today: Gemini four Argon arrives with a price cliff, Mistral Large four weights due this month, and Google's Universal Cart opens agentic checkout.

First, the story of the day.

Google made Gemini 4 Argon generally available on October thirteenth, two weeks after announcing it on September thirtieth and restricting it to vetted cyber defenders. It is served through the paid Gemini API and AI Studio, and in the Gemini app and AI Mode for Pro and Ultra subscribers. The free API tier is excluded. Introductory pricing is two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output tokens, with thinking tokens billed as output. Cached input costs ten cents per million tokens. That introductory rate runs through November thirtieth. From December first, Google's documentation lists four dollars per million input tokens, twenty dollars per million output tokens, and twenty cents per million cached input tokens. Press coverage notes OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra is listed at ten dollars input and fifty dollars output per million tokens.

The read here: this is a pricing cliff disguised as a launch. Any evaluation you run before November thirtieth measures a price you cannot budget on. Compare Argon against alternatives at the December rate, not the promotional one. For a platform team, this is a gateway question. Keep a price table with effective dates per model, and alert when a rate changes. Make sure routing policies are cost-aware, so a doubled price shifts traffic by configuration rather than by a code change. It is also worth watching whether other vendors answer with their own time-limited discounts. Promotional pricing is becoming a standard way to win developer share.

Next, Sovereignty and Europe.

Mistral launched a public preview of Mistral Large 4 on October sixth. It is a mixture-of-experts model of about one trillion parameters, trained on Nvidia GPUs in Mistral's own European datacenters. Weights are promised by the end of October, but no license terms are published yet. List price is one dollar thirty-six per million input tokens and four dollars eighteen per million output tokens. On October nineteenth, Mistral also released Timestral, a family of small time-series models under a modified MIT license.

Why it matters: a European open-weight flagship is only sovereign once you can actually host it. Treat the end-of-month weights and the license as the real test. Timestral is worth a look for a retailer: forecasting models that run on CPU change the inference cost conversation for demand and replenishment.

Next, Platform.

On October thirteenth, NVIDIA released the full Run:ai platform as open source under the Apache 2.0 license. Until now only the KAI Scheduler had been open. The release adds a GPU fractioning operator, resource management for projects and quotas, and a workload map, and includes the control plane. It runs on your own Kubernetes, on premises or in cloud. Enterprise support remains a paid option.

The bigger picture: GPU scheduling, quotas and fractional sharing are no longer behind a licence. For a team running its own inference capacity on Kubernetes, that lowers the cost of fair-share across teams. Worth a proof of concept, though you inherit the operational burden that support contracts used to cover.

Next, Regulation.

The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation 2026/1744, entered into force on July twenty-seventh. It moved stand-alone high-risk obligations from August 2026 to December second, 2027, and embedded-product obligations to August second, 2028. It did not move Article 50 transparency rules for new systems, applicable since August 2026. Two dates still fall on December second, 2026: marking for older generative systems, and two new Article 5 prohibitions.

Reading between the lines: the delay applies to one class of duty only. Your nearest deadline is December second, this year. Check whether any customer-facing generative assistant produces content that needs marking, and whether your gateway can apply it centrally.

Next, Retail and agents.

Google launched Universal Cart in the United States on October fifteenth, across AI Mode in Search and the Gemini app. It holds products from several retailers and checks out through the Universal Commerce Protocol. The merchant stays seller of record. Launch retailers include Target, Walmart and Wayfair. There is no announced date for Europe. Shopify also opened its catalog and Shop Pay checkout to outside agents through an MCP connection.

What this really signals: checkout is moving into the agent, while the retailer keeps fulfilment and the customer service burden. Europe is not live yet, so use the gap to audit what your product data, stock and price APIs would expose to an agent.

The number of the day.

Eighteen of twenty. An October sixteenth audit found that eighteen of twenty large UK online retailers give no explicit robots.txt instructions to AI crawlers or shopping agents, as agents start to shop.

What to watch.

By October thirty-first, Mistral has promised Large 4 weights. Watch the license terms. October twenty-eighth, the Agentic AI Foundation holds a working group on open agentic commerce standards. November thirtieth, Gemini 4 Argon's introductory price ends. January first, Gemini 3.8 Flash rates also double. December second brings the next AI Act dates. Also watch for any European date for Universal Cart.

And a question for your team.

If a model's price doubled next month, could your gateway reroute traffic by configuration alone, and would your evaluations tell you which cheaper model is good enough?

That's it for today. Sources are in the episode description. This was Signal in the AI Fog. See you tomorrow.
