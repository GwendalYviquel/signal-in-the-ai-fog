# Mistral's open-weight deadline and a price cliff

_2026-10-20_

Mistral Large 4 is in preview and its open weights are due within days, while Ecosia's switch to Chinese open models tests the sovereignty pitch. Also: Gemini 4 Argon's December price doubling, Haiku 5.5's token-length price boundary, the December second AI Act marking date, and Google's Universal Cart going live.

## Script

Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 20. This is Signal in the AI Fog, your daily briefing on enterprise AI in production, written and read by AI. Today: Mistral's open weights deadline looms, Gemini 4 Argon price cliff, and Google's Universal Cart goes live.

First, the story of the day.

Mistral put Large 4 into public preview on October sixth. It is a one-trillion-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts model, trained on Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips in Mistral's own European data centers. List price on the API is one dollar thirty-six per million input tokens and four dollars eighteen per million output tokens, with a fifty percent launch discount that was set to run two weeks. The open weights are not out yet. Mistral commits to the end of October, and its chief scientist has pointed to October twenty-seventh. The licence is unpublished; Large 3 shipped under Apache two point zero. Artificial Analysis scores Large 4 at thirty-eight, the best outside the US and China, but behind several Chinese open models. Separately, Ecosia dropped Mistral for hosted Chinese open-weight models, and its CEO says costs roughly halved.

The read here: today, Large 4 is an API preview, not a self-hostable model. For a platform team, sovereignty only becomes real when the weights and the licence are in your hands, so those two items decide everything. The Ecosia story is a warning about method. A vendor's flagship claim and a customer's cost-and-quality result can diverge, especially at scale. Treat both as hypotheses and run your own business benchmark. It is also worth watching whether the licence allows unrestricted commercial self-hosting. If it does, a European open-weight option belongs in your gateway as a routable, evaluated candidate. If it doesn't, the sovereignty argument weakens considerably.

Next, Models and vendors.

Google's Gemini 4 Argon became generally available in the Gemini API on October thirteenth, and on Vertex AI in global, US and EU endpoints. Price is two dollars per million input tokens and ten dollars per million output tokens until November thirtieth. From December first, it doubles to four dollars and twenty dollars. Vertex adds ten percent for regional endpoints.

Reading between the lines: this is a price cliff, not a price. Any business case built on the introductory rate will look twice as expensive in six weeks. Budget at the December figure now, and compare cost per completed task, not sticker price, before you promote it to a default route.

Next, Platform.

Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5, launched October seventh, is tiered by prompt length. Under one hundred thousand tokens it costs ten cents per million input tokens and fifty cents per million output tokens. Above that, fifty cents and two dollars fifty. Anthropic estimates about seventy-five percent average savings versus Haiku 4.5, including a tokenizer that uses slightly more tokens.

What this really signals: prompt length is now a cost dimension. A gateway that routes only on model name and quota will miss a fivefold jump at the threshold. Log token counts per route, and replay your own traffic through the new tokenizer before trusting the headline saving.

Next, Regulation.

Regulation 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI, has been in force since July twenty-seventh. It moves stand-alone high-risk obligations from August 2026 to December second, 2027, and product-embedded ones to August 2028. Article 50 transparency rules apply, and systems already on the market get until December second, 2026 for machine-readable marking of AI-generated content.

Why it matters: the high-risk delay is real, but the December second marking date is about six weeks away. Any retailer generating product images, descriptions or customer-facing text should check now whether outputs carry machine-readable marks, and where in the pipeline that happens.

Next, Retail and agents.

Google launched Universal Cart in the US on October fifteenth, across Search and the Gemini app. It holds items from several retailers, tracks price drops and restocks, and checks out via Google Pay or hands off to the merchant, who stays seller of record. Launch partners include Walmart, Target and Wayfair. YouTube and Gmail have no date.

The bigger picture: the basket moves from the retailer's site to the agent's surface. That makes accurate, machine-readable product, price and stock data the entry ticket. It is worth watching how much checkout partners actually allow, since early reports say some limit it.

The number of the day.

Twenty-one point six percent. That is the share of AI shoppers who have let an assistant complete a purchase, according to Product.ai research. Nearly ninety percent still verify recommendations before buying.

What to watch.

October twenty-seventh is the reported date for Mistral Large 4's open weights. Watch the licence terms as much as the files. Mistral's launch discount was set to end around now. Looking further out, December first brings Gemini 4 Argon's doubled price, and December second is the AI Act marking date for generative systems already on the market. Also watch whether Google dates its YouTube and Gmail cart.

And a question for your team.

If a new open-weight model lands next week, can your evaluation harness score it against your current default on your own business tasks within forty-eight hours?

That's it for today. Sources are in the episode description. This was Signal in the AI Fog. See you tomorrow.
